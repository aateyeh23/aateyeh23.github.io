Bio

I am a second-year PhD student in Statistics at UC Berkeley, supported by an NSF Graduate Research Fellowship. My research combines causal inference with the theory of modern machine learning, with the goal of making AI a reliable tool for scientific discovery and decision-making. Recent work studies which learning algorithms transformers can implement through continuous latent reasoning, and how structured attention masks trade off expressivity and efficiency in long-context models.

Before Berkeley, I spent two years as a quantitative trader at the Voleon Group, a machine-learning hedge fund, where I worked on portfolio risk, automated hedging, and securities lending. That work drew me to the foundational questions behind the statistical tools I used every day: why they work, and when their conclusions can be trusted.

I received a BS in Applied and Computational Mathematics from Caltech in 2023. I grew up in rural Kentucky, and I care about widening access to statistics: at Berkeley I mentor undergraduates through the Statistical Undergraduate Mentorship (SUM) program and serve on the Graduate Assembly.
